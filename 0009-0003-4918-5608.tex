\begin{abstract}
The coconut palm (\textit{Cocos nucifera} L.) is an essential pillar of tropical agricultural economies, dietary security, and export revenue. However, crop yields face severe threats from aggressive biological pathogens, notably Weligama Coconut Leaf Wilt Disease (WCLWD), Bud Rot, Stem Bleeding, and Leaf Rot. Existing monitoring approaches suffer from a scale bifurcation gap: high-altitude aerial surveillance via Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) provides macroscopic coverage but lacks lesion-level diagnostic resolution, whereas mobile deep learning classifiers operate in isolation and depend on erratic network connectivity. This paper presents an end-to-end, multiscale computer vision ecosystem bridging macroscopic aerial surveillance with offline-first microscopic edge intelligence. At the macroscopic tier (System~A), deterministic mathematical morphology, integrating Canonical Excess-Green (ExG) chromatic canopy isolation, Euclidean Distance Transform (EDT) topological crown extraction, and a Moving-Window Local Spatial Z-Score Anomaly Engine, enables a zero-annotation physical tree census and early stress hotspot localization without deep neural network overhead. At the microscopic tier (System~B), an asymmetric Full-Integer INT8 Post-Training Quantized MobileNetV2 architecture (2.71~MB) combined with Temperature-Scaled Shannon Entropy Out-of-Distribution (OOD) gating delivers rapid foliar diagnostics on budget mobile processors while rejecting non-foliar visual noise. Diagnostic telemetry is orchestrated through a hierarchical biosecurity bridge combining local SQLite offline queuing with asynchronous Firebase synchronization and Coconut Research Institute (CRI) treatment protocols. Empirical validations across commercial plantations (Ambakele Research Station) demonstrate rapid crown extraction (938 discrete palms isolated in 100.9ms, 97.6\% F1 on a 1.0~ha audit quadrat), localized stress hotspot detection within a 35m spatial radius (21 early stress hotspots at $Z < -2.0$), and a 91.42\% Top-1 diagnostic accuracy across pathological classes (Cohen's $\kappa = 0.8954$, 95\% CI: [87.55\%, 94.85\%]) on a leak-free un-augmented field benchmark ($N=233$, 100\% recall on Healthy baseline), delivering an affordable and robust biosecurity architecture for smallholder precision agriculture.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The coconut palm (\textit{Cocos nucifera} L.) is a major agricultural commodity in tropical developing nations. In Sri Lanka, coconut cultivation covers approximately 402,649 hectares (representing 21\% of total agricultural land), primarily clustered in the traditional ``Coconut Triangle'' and southern ``Mini Coconut Triangle'' \cite{b1}. Despite its economic value, the industry faces severe stability challenges driven by weather variability, estate land fragmentation, and aggressive biological pathogens.

Among these threats, Weligama Coconut Leaf Wilt Disease (WCLWD), a phytoplasma-mediated systemic disorder closely related to sugarcane grassy shoot phytoplasma, has caused widespread yield losses in southern Sri Lanka \cite{b5}. Because early phytoplasma symptoms involve subtle leaflet flaccidity and uneven chlorosis, they frequently mimic benign magnesium and nitrogen deficiencies, misleading visual inspections \cite{b11,b18}. Concurrently, lethal fungal and oomycete infections such as Bud Rot (\textit{Phytophthora palmivora}), Stem Bleeding (\textit{Ceratocystis paradoxa}), and Leaf Rot (\textit{Colletotrichum gloeosporioides}) damage internal vascular tissues and apical meristems before outer canopy collapse is visible. In severe outbreaks, phytosanitary authorities such as the Coconut Research Institute (CRI) mandate the felling of infected palms to contain transmission \cite{b6}.

Current agronomic monitoring protocols face two primary structural bottlenecks, termed here as the \textbf{Scale Bifurcation Gap}:
\begin{enumerate}[\IEEEsetlabelwidth{1)}]
    \setlength{\itemsep}{0.5pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textit{Macroscopic Disconnect:} High-altitude Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) can survey 50--100 hectare estates rapidly \cite{b10,b13}. However, deploying standard deep learning object detectors (e.g., YOLOv11, Mask R-CNN) on 20-megapixel drone orthomosaics requires extensive manual bounding-box annotations, induces memory exhaustion on serverless cloud functions, and cannot verify microscopic pathogen etiologies beneath the crown.
    \item \textit{Microscopic Isolation:} Ground-level mobile deep learning models enable lesion-level diagnosis \cite{b3,b9}. However, they function in spatial isolation, rely on uncoordinated manual patrols, and fail in rural zero-connectivity zones. Furthermore, standard softmax activations exhibit uncalibrated overconfidence on non-foliar field noise (mud, fingers, glare) \cite{b12,b19}.
\end{enumerate}

To resolve these challenges, this paper presents a multiscale computer vision ecosystem uniting macroscopic aerial surveillance with offline-first microscopic edge diagnostics. The core contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \setlength{\itemsep}{0.5pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{Deterministic Macroscopic Topology (System A):} A lightweight mathematical morphology pipeline combining Canonical Excess-Green (ExG) chromatic filtering and Euclidean Distance Transform (EDT) peak extraction that achieves an automated physical tree census without annotated neural network training data.
    \item \textbf{Local Spatial Z-Score Anomaly Engine:} A moving-window spatial statistical filter that isolates early canopy stress clusters against local neighborhood baseline vigor, mitigating background soil reflectance bias and spatial autocorrelation.
    \item \textbf{Quantized Edge Diagnostics with Calibrated OOD Gating (System B):} An asymmetric Full-Integer INT8 Post-Training Quantized MobileNetV2 model (disk footprint compressed by 75.0\% to 2.71~MB) integrated with Temperature-Scaled Shannon Entropy gating to filter non-foliar visual artifacts as ``Ambiguous Foliage''.
    \item \textbf{Statistically Validated Benchmark Corpus:} An expanded, leak-free multi-class corpus of 3,691 images with source-partitioned blind testing ($N=233$), 5-fold cross-validation ($90.8\% \pm 1.4\%$), 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals, and McNemar quantization fidelity testing.
    \item \textbf{Hierarchical Field Dispatch Bridge:} An asynchronous orchestration protocol linking macroscopic UAV hotspot coordinates to mobile field-scout GPS dispatches, supported by an offline-first SQLite synchronization engine and CRI agronomic remediation protocols.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work \& Phytopathological Context}

\subsection{Deep Learning in Foliar Pathology and Edge Vision}
Recent studies highlight the efficacy of deep convolutional networks for foliar disease classification. Daga et al. \cite{b1} proposed DeepSeqCoco, an optimized mobile architecture using hybrid SGD-Adam optimization that reduced inference latency on edge devices. Alkhawaji et al. \cite{b9} and Singh et al. \cite{b10} evaluated deep convolutional backbones for palm disease classification, reporting high validation accuracies on curated benchmarks. Similarly, the hybrid NNSVCLD framework \cite{b3} combined deep feature extraction with Support Vector Machines (SVM) to classify bud rot and leaf blight with 99.4\% accuracy. Broader plant pathology benchmarks, such as PlantVillage \cite{b21}, have established the feasibility of deep transfer learning across foliar datasets.

However, heavy architectures (e.g., EfficientNet-B7 or Inception-ResNet-V2 \cite{b12}) cause thermal throttling and battery drain on budget smartphone processors. Furthermore, standard mobile classifiers lack out-of-distribution (OOD) rejection mechanisms, producing uncalibrated, overconfident predictions on non-foliar visual artifacts \cite{b19,b20}. Integer quantization frameworks (e.g., Jacob et al. \cite{b22} and MobileNetV3 \cite{b23}) have demonstrated that fixed-point arithmetic can significantly compress model footprint while preserving numerical fidelity on edge hardware.

\subsection{Aerial Remote Sensing in Plantation Agriculture}
At the macroscopic canopy scale, UAV multispectral imaging combined with Object-Based Image Analysis (OBIA) has shown efficacy in detecting canopy stress \cite{b13}. Divyanth et al. \cite{b13} demonstrated that Near-Infrared (NIR) vegetation indices isolate internal cellular mesophyll collapse in WCLWD-affected palms prior to visual chlorosis. Advanced segmentation models such as JASS-Net \cite{b14} and TKRnet \cite{b15} achieve strong canopy delineation. However, executing sliding-window neural inference over high-resolution drone orthomosaics requires dedicated GPU cloud infrastructure, making continuous deployment economically prohibitive for smallholder cooperatives.

\subsection{Etiological Modeling: A Multiscale Disease Complex}
Phytopathological management in Sri Lanka requires modeling diseases across spatial scales. WCLWD manifests macroscopically through canopy thinning, flaccidity of the lower frond whorls, and generalized chlorosis. However, secondary foliar necrosis and opportunistic fungal blights (specifically \textit{Colletotrichum} Leaf Rot and \textit{Pestalotiopsis} Gray Leaf Spot) rapidly colonize immunocompromised palms. Consequently, a robust biosecurity framework must detect canopy vigor decline aerially and subsequently verify the specific active necrotic agent on the ground across a verified 6-class pathological taxonomy: (1) Bud Rot, (2) Stem Bleeding, (3) Leaf Rot, (4) Bud Root Dropping, (5) Gray Leaf Spot, and (6) Healthy Baseline.

\subsection{UAV Flight Acquisition and Photogrammetry}
Aerial orthomosaics were captured over commercial coconut blocks at the Ambakele Research Station in Sri Lanka using a DJI Mavic 3 Multispectral (M3M) enterprise UAV. The platform integrates a 4/3-inch CMOS 20~MP RGB sensor ($5280 \times 3956$ pixels, mechanical shutter) and a four-band multispectral array ($5\text{ MP}$ per band: Green at $560 \pm 16\text{ nm}$, Red at $650 \pm 16\text{ nm}$, Red Edge at $730 \pm 16\text{ nm}$, and Near-Infrared at $860 \pm 26\text{ nm}$). Missions were executed at a flight altitude of $100\text{ m}$ Above Ground Level (AGL) with $80\%$ forward overlap and $70\%$ side overlap under solar zenith conditions ($10\text{:}00\text{--}12\text{:}00\text{ hrs}$), yielding a Ground Sample Distance (GSD) of $4.8\text{ cm/pixel}$. Downwelling solar irradiance was calibrated continuously via an integrated real-time sunshine sensor, and orthomosaics were reconstructed using Structure-from-Motion (SfM) photogrammetry in DJI Terra.

\subsection{Strategy A: Radiometric Canopy Isolation}
Commercial coconut plantations in Sri Lanka adhere to an $8.0\text{m} \times 8.0\text{m}$ grid, causing 60\% to 70\% of raw aerial imagery to consist of non-canopy background (sandy loam soil, gravel roads, drying yards, and shadows). Unfiltered global index calculations severely depress mean spectral values due to soil reflectance.

Raw RGB channels ($R, G, B \in [0, 255]$) are normalized into chromatic coordinates $r = R/\Sigma_{\text{rgb}}, g = G/\Sigma_{\text{rgb}}, b = B/\Sigma_{\text{rgb}}$ where $\Sigma_{\text{rgb}} = R+G+B$. The Normalized Excess-Green Index ($\text{ExG}$) is derived to amplify photosynthetic tissue dominance:
\begin{equation}
\text{ExG} = 2g - r - b
\label{eq:exg}
\end{equation}
A binary Canonical Physical Canopy Mask $\mathcal{M}(x,y) \in \{0, 1\}$ is generated via empirical gating and Otsu global binarization:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{M}(x,y) = 
\begin{cases}
1, & \text{if } \text{ExG}(x,y) \ge \tau_{\text{otsu}} \;\wedge\; g(x,y) > r(x,y) \\
0, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:canopy_mask}
\end{equation}
All non-canopy pixels ($\mathcal{M}=0$) are mapped to a neutral zero-reflectance dark slate tensor, resolving soil reflectance bias while reducing downstream processing memory.

\subsection{Strategy B: Deterministic Morphological Crown Extraction}
System~A exploits the radial canopy symmetry of the monocot palm. For all confirmed canopy pixels in $\mathcal{M}$, the Euclidean Distance Transform (EDT) computes the shortest distance to the nearest background boundary:
\begin{equation}
D(p) = \min_{q \notin \mathcal{M}} \|p - q\|_2, \quad \forall p \in \mathcal{M}
\label{eq:edt}
\end{equation}
Because fronds radiate outward from the apical meristem, $D(p)$ generates a topological cone whose local maximum coincides with the crown center. Gaussian topological smoothing is applied:
\begin{equation}
D_{\text{smooth}} = D * G_\sigma, \quad G_\sigma = \frac{1}{2\pi\sigma^2} \exp\left(-\frac{x^2+y^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)
\label{eq:gaussian}
\end{equation}
where scale factor $\sigma = 2.2$ is analytically calibrated: values of $\sigma < 1.5$ preserve intra-frond leaflet ridges (over-segmentation), whereas $\sigma > 3.5$ merges interlocking crowns from adjacent palms.

Individual palm apices $\mathcal{P}$ are extracted using a Spatial Local Maxima Peak filter over an agronomic footprint $\mathcal{F}$ of size $(2d_{\min} + 1) \times (2d_{\min} + 1)$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{P} = \left\{ p \in \mathcal{M} \;\middle|\; D_{\text{smooth}}(p) = \max_{q \in \mathcal{F}(p)} D_{\text{smooth}}(q) \;\wedge\; D_{\text{smooth}}(p) \ge \delta_{\text{depth}} \right\}
\label{eq:peaks}
\end{equation}
where $d_{\min} \approx 26\text{ pixels}$ corresponds directly to the physical $8.0\text{m}$ CRI planting distance under standard orthomosaic Ground Sample Distance (GSD $\sim 4.8\text{ cm/px}$), and $\delta_{\text{depth}} = 6.0\text{ pixels}$ excludes isolated weed patches. This deterministic process produces an immutable geospatial coordinate $(\text{lat}_i, \text{lng}_i)$ and crown radius $r_i$ for every physical tree without manual annotations.

\subsection{Spectral Index Formulations}
For RGB imagery, Visible Atmospherically Resistant Index (VARI) estimates vegetative vigor while compensating for aerosol scattering; for multispectral sensors, Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) detects cellular mesophyll decay:
\begin{equation}
\text{VARI} = \frac{G - R}{G + R - B}, \qquad \text{NDVI} = \frac{\text{NIR} - R}{\text{NIR} + R}
\label{eq:indices}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Strategy C: Moving-Window Local Spatial Z-Score Engine}
Pathological infection spreads in localized clusters, but plantations also exhibit broad spatial trends in soil fertility and moisture (spatial autocorrelation). System~A evaluates each palm $i \in \mathcal{P}$ against its spatial neighborhood $\mathcal{N}_i$ within radius $R \approx 35\text{m}$ to $50\text{m}$:
\begin{equation}
\mu_i = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{N}_i|} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i} S_j, \quad \sigma_i = \sqrt{\frac{1}{|\mathcal{N}_i|} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i} (S_j - \mu_i)^2}
\label{eq:local_stats}
\end{equation}
where $S_j$ is the mean spectral index across the canopy disk of tree $j$. The individual tree anomaly Z-Score is derived as:
\begin{equation}
Z_i = \frac{S_i - \mu_i}{\sigma_i + \epsilon}
\label{eq:zscore}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$ is a standard machine-precision regularizer preventing division by zero. By normalizing each palm against its local baseline $(\mu_i, \sigma_i)$, the engine de-trends broad spatial autocorrelation (Moran's spatial lag). Palms exhibiting $Z_i < -2.0$ (representing the lower $2.28\%$ extreme statistical tail, $p < 0.023$) are classified as acute stress outliers and serialized as active canopy hotspots in Firestore.

\section{System B: Microscopic Mobile Edge Diagnostics}

\subsection{MobileNetV2 Quantization Architecture}
Ground verification in disconnected plantation environments demands efficient edge inference. System~B utilizes MobileNetV2 ($\alpha=1.0$, input size $224 \times 224 \times 3$) built on 17 inverted residual bottleneck blocks with depthwise separable convolutions \cite{b10}. Pointwise $1\times1$ expansions project feature manifolds, $3\times3$ depthwise convolutions perform spatial filtering, and $1\times1$ linear bottlenecks project representations back to low-dimensional subspaces without non-linear information loss.

To eliminate floating-point execution overhead, the trained model is subjected to Asymmetric Full-Integer INT8 Post-Training Quantization (PTQ). Continuous 32-bit floating-point tensors $r \in [\alpha, \beta]$ are mapped to 8-bit signed integers $q \in [-128, 127]$:
\begin{equation}
q = \text{clamp}\left( \left\lfloor \frac{r}{S} \right\rceil + Z, -128, 127 \right)
\label{eq:quant}
\end{equation}
where scale factor $S = (\beta - \alpha)/255$ and integer zero-point offset $Z = \text{round}(-\alpha/S) - 128$. Real-valued matrix multipliers are decomposed into fixed-point integer mantissas and arithmetic bit-shifts, executing directly on 32-bit integer ALU registers.

\subsection{Temperature-Scaled Shannon Entropy OOD Gating}
To prevent uncalibrated overconfidence on out-of-distribution non-foliar visual noise (mud, skin, sun glare) \cite{b19}, System~B implements a Temperature-Scaled Softmax gating layer. Logit outputs $z_k$ ($k=1,\dots,K$) are calibrated via temperature parameter $T = 1.5$:
\begin{equation}
p_k = \frac{\exp(z_k / T)}{\sum_{j=1}^K \exp(z_j / T)}, \qquad H(P) = -\sum_{k=1}^K p_k \log_2(p_k)
\label{eq:temp_softmax}
\end{equation}
For $K=6$ classes, maximum entropy occurs under uniform uncertainty ($H_{\max} = \log_2(6) \approx 2.585\text{ bits}$). An agronomically reliable diagnosis requires top-class confidence $\max(P) \ge 0.60$, corresponding to $H \approx 2.10\text{ bits}$ ($81.2\%$ of $H_{\max}$). The gating decision rule rejects ambiguous or non-foliar inputs:
\begin{equation}
\hat{y} = 
\begin{cases}
\text{Reject}, & \text{if } H(P) > H_{\text{th}} \;\vee\; \max(P) < \tau \\
\arg\max_k(p_k), & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:rejection}
\end{equation}
where $H_{\text{th}} = 2.1\text{ bits}$ and $\tau = 0.40$. Rejected images trigger immediate on-device guidance prompting the field scout to capture a clearer sample from an adjusted viewpoint.

\subsection{Dataset Curation and Training Setup}
The model was trained on an expanded, leak-free multi-class corpus of 3,691 field-annotated images across six target classes. Data partitioning was strictly enforced at the physical source image level prior to offline training augmentation: 3,225 training images, 233 validation images, and 233 blind testing images. Crucially, the 233 test images represent 100\% un-augmented, unseen field captures across all six classes (Bud Root Dropping: 31, Bud Rot: 37, Gray Leaf Spot: 40, Healthy Baseline: 20, Leaf Rot: 48, Stem Bleeding: 57). Stratified 5-fold cross-validation on the training corpus yielded a mean accuracy of $90.8\% \pm 1.4\%$.

Input images were normalized into $[-1, 1]$ ($224 \times 224 \times 3$). Training utilized the Adam optimizer ($\beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.999$, weight decay $10^{-4}$) with initial learning rate $\eta_{\text{initial}} = 10^{-4}$ decayed via cosine annealing down to $\eta_{\min} = 10^{-6}$ over 15 epochs (batch size 32). Cross-entropy loss was weighted inversely proportional to class frequencies: $w_c = N_{\text{total}} / (K \cdot N_c)$ where $K=6$. Representative calibration for INT8 PTQ utilized 250 diverse unlabelled plantation samples to establish activation dynamic range boundaries $[\alpha_{\min}, \beta_{\max}]$ \cite{b22}. Training augmentations included random rotations ($\pm 180^\circ$), perspective warps, color jittering (brightness $\pm 25\%$, contrast $\pm 20\%$), and cutout occlusions.

\section{Hierarchical Field Dispatch Bridge}

\subsection{Cloud-to-Mobile Deployment Architecture}
The biosecurity ecosystem operates via an asynchronous, multi-tiered cloud-and-edge bridge designed for cost-effective deployment across resource-constrained cooperatives:
\begin{enumerate}[\IEEEsetlabelwidth{1)}]
    \setlength{\itemsep}{0.5pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textit{Cloud Ingestion \& Processing:} Drone orthomosaics are processed serverlessly on Google Cloud Functions (Python 3.11, headless OpenCV/SciPy). System~A extracts canopy stress hotspots ($Z_i < -2.0$) with georeferenced coordinates in sub-second CPU time.
    \item \textit{Centralized Orchestration:} Hotspots are committed to Google Cloud Firestore under \texttt{canopy\_hotspots/\{id\}} with attributes: \texttt{[lat, lng, z\_score, ndvi\_mean, status, timestamp, scout\_id]}. Firestore's Spark Free Tier (50k daily reads, 20k writes) supports estate monitoring without operational cloud costs.
    \item \textit{Mobile Dispatch Engine:} The field scout application is built on React Native with TypeScript and integrates Google LiteRT (TensorFlow Lite C++ interface), querying pending hotspots sorted by spatial Euclidean distance to the scout's device GPS.
    \item \textit{Edge Diagnosis \& CRI Interventions:} The scout captures foliar/trunk imagery, executes INT8 inference, and accesses embedded CRI treatment protocols (Table~\ref{tab:cri_matrix}).
    \item \textit{Offline-First SQLite Synchronization:} In rural zero-connectivity zones, telemetry is serialized locally using SQLite (`expo-sqlite`). A background listener triggers parallel batch synchronization via Firebase \texttt{BulkWriter} upon network restoration.
\end{enumerate}

To validate tree extraction fidelity, a two-tier evaluation was conducted:
\begin{enumerate}[\IEEEsetlabelwidth{1)}]
    \setlength{\itemsep}{0.5pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textit{Agronomic Spatial Density Consistency:} On the CRI standardized $8.0\text{m} \times 8.0\text{m}$ planting grid ($\approx 156\text{ palms/ha}$), the surveyed $6.0\text{ ha}$ block has a theoretical capacity of $\approx 936\text{ palms}$. The algorithm extracted $938\text{ discrete crowns}$ in $100.9\text{ ms}$ ($99.8\%$ agreement with physical plantation capacity).
    \item \textit{Sub-Quadrat Visual Audit:} Manual audit across a $1.0\text{ ha}$ sample quadrat containing 150 known palm coordinates yielded 146 true positives ($TP$), 3 false positives ($FP$, interlocking weeds), and 4 false negatives ($FN$, stunted replants), achieving $\textbf{97.3\% Recall}$, $\textbf{98.0\% Precision}$, and $\textbf{97.6\% F1-Score}$.
\end{enumerate}

Parametric ablation confirmed that $\sigma=2.2$ optimally balances intra-frond leaflet suppression and inter-canopy separation: under-smoothed EDT ($\sigma=1.0$) generated 1,507 spurious peaks (leaflet over-segmentation), while over-smoothed EDT ($\sigma=4.0$) merged adjacent crowns into 508 peaks. Unsegmented blob analysis generated 4,577 fragmented components due to intertwined canopy boundaries (Table~\ref{tab:system_a_comparison}). The underlying radial topological invariant directly generalizes to other radially symmetric monocots, including Oil Palm (\textit{Elaeis guineensis}) and Date Palm (\textit{Phoenix dactylifera}), by adjusting footprint $d_{\min}$.

Across multispectral NDVI channels, the Moving-Window Local Spatial Z-Score engine identified $21\text{ early stress hotspots}$ ($Z < -2.0$) within a $35\text{m}$ local neighborhood ($2.24\%$ of palms), isolating sub-visual mesophyll decay cross-verified by field agronomic reports. Sensitivity analysis indicated that $Z < -1.5$ increases alerts to $6.8\%$ (incorporating benign shade variations), while $Z < -2.5$ detects only $0.6\%$ (omitting early infections). In contrast, global NDVI thresholding flagged $>18.4\%$ of the plantation as false positive outliers due to background soil reflectance and topographical dry-edge bias.

\subsection{Microscopic Classification Performance}
The quantized INT8 MobileNetV2 model was benchmarked against the blind, un-augmented field test set ($N=233$ pure base images across all 6 classes). As summarized in Table~\ref{tab:metrics}, the edge model achieved an overall Top-1 accuracy of $91.42\%$ and Cohen's $\kappa = 0.8954$. Compared to heavier backbones such as ResNet-50 ($98\text{ MB}$, $\sim 280\text{ ms}$ mobile latency) and EfficientNet-B0 ($21\text{ MB}$, $\sim 140\text{ ms}$), the quantized MobileNetV2 delivers competitive diagnostic accuracy at a fraction of the compute and memory footprint ($2.71\text{ MB}$, $11.76\text{ ms}$ CPU latency).

Across individual classes, the model demonstrated high sensitivity on lethal conditions: Bud Rot ($100.0\%$ Recall, $90.24\%$ F1, $N=37$), Bud Root Dropping ($100.0\%$ Precision, $98.36\%$ F1, $N=31$), Stem Bleeding ($100.0\%$ Precision, $99.12\%$ F1, $N=57$), and Healthy Baseline ($100.0\%$ Recall, $88.89\%$ F1, $N=20$). Gray Leaf Spot ($86.11\%$ F1, $N=40$) and Leaf Rot ($83.87\%$ F1, $N=48$) exhibited expected minor foliar boundary confusion. Macro-averaged precision, recall, and F1-score reached $90.96\%$, $92.29\%$, and $91.10\%$ respectively.

Bootstrap resampling (1,000 iterations) yielded a 95\% confidence interval for Top-1 accuracy of $[87.55\%, 94.85\%]$, macro F1-score of $[86.91\%, 94.65\%]$, and Cohen's Kappa of $[0.8501, 0.9368]$. McNemar's statistical significance test between the FP32 baseline (89.70\%) and the INT8 quantized model (91.42\%) yielded $p = 0.2188 > 0.05$, confirming zero statistically significant diagnostic loss from integer quantization.

\subsection{Out-of-Distribution Gating Validation}
The Temperature-Scaled Shannon Entropy filter was evaluated on a dual non-foliar challenge dataset ($N=500$) comprising 250 in-situ field non-foliar captures (soil, hands, boots, tools, trunk bark) and 250 extreme radiometric noise variations (solar flare, lens blur, underexposure). Uncalibrated baseline Softmax falsely assigned high-confidence disease diagnoses ($>80\%$ confidence) to 81.4\% of noise inputs. In contrast, the Shannon Entropy gate ($T=1.5, H_{\text{th}}=2.1$) correctly rejected 100.0\% of non-foliar artifacts as ``Ambiguous Foliage'', eliminating false positive alerts in operational plantation conditions.

\subsection{Benchmark Fidelity, Controlled Abstention, and Generalization}
The experimental results demonstrate that the INT8 quantized MobileNetV2 architecture achieves robust diagnostic fidelity (91.42\% Top-1 accuracy, Cohen's $\kappa = 0.8954$) on the leak-free, un-augmented field benchmark of 233 unseen base images. Practical field deployment in unconstrained plantation environments is subject to environmental domain shifts: factors such as extreme tropical solar glare, deep canopy shadows, camera lens smudges, motion blur on low-cost smartphones, and complex co-infections introduce visual ambiguities.

The observed accuracy range (87.55\% to 94.85\% within the 95\% confidence interval) aligns with field operational expectations (88\%--92\%). In biosecurity diagnostics, an uncalibrated false-confident prediction (a ``silent failure'') risks unchecked pathogen transmission or unnecessary tree destruction. The Temperature-Scaled Shannon Entropy OOD gating mechanism enforces a \textit{Controlled Abstention} safety protocol: when presented with degraded or out-of-distribution samples, the system intercepts the prediction and provides immediate interactive feedback, enabling field scouts to adjust camera angles in-situ.

\section{Conclusion \& Future Work}
This paper presented a multiscale computer vision ecosystem bridging macroscopic UAV canopy surveillance with offline-first microscopic edge diagnostics for coconut pathology management. By replacing heavy neural networks at the macroscopic scale with deterministic Euclidean Distance Transform topology and localized Moving-Window Z-Score anomaly filtering, the architecture achieves rapid palm tree extraction (938 crowns in 100.9ms, 97.6\% F1 on 1.0~ha audit) and robust early stress localization. At the microscopic edge, an INT8 quantized MobileNetV2 classifier coupled with Shannon Entropy out-of-distribution gating achieves a 91.42\% overall accuracy (Cohen's $\kappa = 0.8954$, 95\% CI: [87.55\%, 94.85\%], 91.10\% macro F1) with 11.76ms latency on CPU (68.2ms on budget mobile processors) on a leak-free field benchmark ($N=233$). Telemetry synchronization via an offline-first SQLite queue and bidirectional Firebase bridge ensures biosecurity coordination across bandwidth-scarce rural estates. Future work will investigate direct on-device C++ JavaScript Interface (JSI) model execution and multi-temporal satellite thermal integration for regional drought-pathogen co-analysis. Upon acceptance, model weights and evaluation code will be open-sourced to support reproducible research.
